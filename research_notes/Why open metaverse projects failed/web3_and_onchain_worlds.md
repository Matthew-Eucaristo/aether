# Web3 metaverses and on-chain "autonomous worlds": outcomes, causes, and what a chain can realistically hold

Research date: 2026-11-24. Budget-limited pass (about 18 tool calls). Items marked **[background, not re-verified this session]** come from prior knowledge with a canonical URL but were not fetched in this pass; the report writer should treat them as lower confidence. ## Part A: What happened to the web3 metaverses (case files with numbers)

### Takeaway
Every land-sale-first web3 world lost 90-99% of token value from the 2021 peak and has a small user base, from hundreds to a few thousand daily. Several are restructuring or winding down. The causes repeat across projects: a speculative economy launched before any gameplay, artificial land scarcity that left the map empty, and on-chain "users" that were mostly wallets trading. Mojang's 2022 ban sets out the opposing value system explicitly: scarcity and exclusion, it said, are incompatible with a shared creative world.

### Inferences
- **Speculation first, world second.** Decentraland, The Sandbox, Otherside and Illuvium sold land or tokens years before they had compelling gameplay. Early holders were investors, not players. When prices fell, the people with the strongest incentive to build or visit left.
- **Scarce land produces empty worlds.** A finite parcel map, bought by speculators who don't build, guarantees large unbuilt or idle areas. Minecraft and Roblox do the opposite: unlimited free space, where the scarce thing is attention and creativity, not coordinates. A Cardinal/OASIS-style world should not sell space.
- **Wallet activity is not usage.** The 38-DAU dispute shows that on-chain metrics count transactions, not presence. Most presence in these worlds was never on-chain anyway. Even "on-chain metaverses" ran avatars and chat on ordinary servers.
- **"Decentralized" components were often the weakest link.** Ronin's bridge was a small multisig. DAO-governed worlds still depended on a foundation that held treasury tokens and ran the servers.
- **Mojang's statement is a design constraint, not only a ban.** If the founder's web3 layer creates tradeable scarcity, the largest sandbox community will reject it on principle. Identity and anti-capture are compatible with Mojang's values. Scarce tradeable items are not.

### Gaps
- No verified 2026 DAU for The Sandbox, Somnium, Otherside, Star Atlas or Illuvium. Aggregator figures are unsourced.
- Peak land-sale records (for example the about $2.4M Decentraland parcel and about $4.3M Sandbox parcel in late 2021) and current floor prices were not verified this session.
- Somnium Space shutdown claim unverified. Exact date of the Sandbox layoff reports unverified.
- Total VC funding per project not systematically collected. Known: Hytopia $8M node sale, Star Atlas $7-12.2M (conflicting).

## Part B: Technically serious on-chain / autonomous worlds

### Takeaway
The serious autonomous-world projects proved that small, turn-based or slow-tick game state can live fully on-chain and keep running without its creators. None proved that a real-time, many-entity world can, and the flagship infrastructure company did not survive. Lattice, maker of MUD and Redstone, announced its wind-down in April 2026 and shut Redstone down on 2026-05-15, saying it "never found a sustainable business." Its main game, DUST, moved to a separate chain. This is the most important case for the founder: an "on-chain" world is only as durable as the chain and data-availability layer under it.

**What goes on-chain vs off-chain, tick rates, costs** (synthesis of the above plus background)
- In MUD and Dojo, all canonical game state (ECS tables or components) is in contract storage. Clients read through indexers (MUD store-sync / Dozer, Dojo Torii). Rendering, input prediction and chat stay off-chain.
- Tick rate = block time. Ethereum L1 has 12 s slots. OP Stack chains such as Base and Redstone have about 2 s blocks. Real-time experiments (Quarry 7 ms confirmations, MegaETH about 10 ms "mini-blocks") reach game-like latency only by having a single sequencer issue preconfirmations. — [lattice.xyz](https://lattice.xyz/); [Blockonomi on MegaETH](https://blockonomi.com/megaeth-launches-real-time-ethereum-l2-with-sub-10ms-blocks-and-89m-tvl/)
- Games built on these chains kept state changes to discrete, player-initiated actions such as moves, builds and attacks. Lazy evaluation (computing the result when queried, rather than every tick) and turn-based designs avoid per-tick writes.

### Inferences
- **Redstone is the founder's cautionary tale.** It was an "autonomous worlds" chain whose operator ran out of money. Because it used alt-DA (data not posted to L1), durability after shutdown depended on someone else keeping the data. Fortunately, MUD's migration tool and open-source code let DUST move. Lesson: the forkability and exportability of state and code mattered more than the chain did.
- **Autonomy has not prevented abandonment.** Sky Strife and Loot keep "running" in principle, but they stay alive only through community interest. Being on-chain secures existence, not activity.
- **A single sequencer is a single operator.** Real-time chains (Quarry, MegaETH, game appchains) reach low latency by centralizing ordering. For "no one can take over the world", a game appchain with one sequencer is roughly as capturable as a server, unless there are forced-inclusion and escape hatches through L1 and the data is on L1 or a robust DA layer.
- **Hidden information, AI agents and physics** all fit poorly on public chains. Dark Forest needed zk-SNARKs for one mechanic. A Cardinal-style AI game master running LLMs cannot execute on-chain; at best, its outputs can be committed on-chain.

## Part C: Feasibility for an AI-native, interface-agnostic world with on-chain accounts and object state

### Takeaway
Storing the live world's object state on-chain at 20-60 Hz is not feasible on any decentralized chain in 2026. A 1,000-entity world at 30 Hz is about 30,000 state writes per second. That is above Base's real-world peak of about 160 TPS and only approached by single-sequencer "real-time" L2s such as MegaETH, which recentralize the very thing the founder wants to protect. The realistic architecture has three parts:
- **On the chain:** a thin layer for identity, ownership and a registry.
- **In content-addressed storage:** world snapshots, assets and scripts.
- **Off-chain:** a deterministic, open-source authoritative simulation that periodically commits a state root to the chain and can be re-executed by anyone, which makes it forkable and challengeable.

Federation protocols, especially AT Protocol with DIDs and repo-based account portability, are a better model for "no one can destroy it" than DAO token voting. Token governance has a documented record of capture.

### Cited Findings

**Throughput and latency, 2026**
- Base runs about 2 s blocks. Real-world peak of about 159 TPS (April 2026 analysis). Compute about 26-60 MGas/s, with a stated target of 1,000 MGas/s. — [crawlux MegaETH vs Base](https://www.crawlux.com/compare/megaeth-vs-base/); [crawlux best L2](https://www.crawlux.com/blog/best-ethereum-l2/) (secondary; figures approximate)

Per-transaction gas limit is about 1B, versus about 30M on L1, Arbitrum and Base. Specialized single-sequencer design. — [Yellow research](https://yellow.com/research/megaeth-launch-10000-tps-layer-2-performance-2026); [Blockonomi](https://blockonomi.com/megaeth-launches-real-time-ethereum-l2-with-sub-10ms-blocks-and-89m-tvl/); [bex.co](https://bex.co/blog/2026/02/09/megaeth-mainnet-real-time-blockchain-100k-tps)

- World Engine claimed sub-100 ms blocks per game shard (2024 marketing). — [Argus blog](https://blog.argus.gg/world-engine-launch)

**Federation and portability: AT Protocol (Bluesky)**
- Account migration: create an account on a new PDS, move the repo data, update identity through a signed did:plc operation, and finalize. The guide says these mechanisms "are not a formal part of the protocol, and may evolve." — [atproto.com Account Migration](https://atproto.com/guides/account-migration)
- did:plc rotation keys are ranked. A user can add a self-held rotation key above the PDS's key, which lets them override a hostile host. This is the "credible exit" mechanism. Rotation keys cover identity only; data needs backups. — [atproto.com Account Recovery](https://atproto.com/guides/account-recovery); [jola.dev walkthrough](https://jola.dev/posts/taking-control-atproto-account)
- Caveats: Bluesky warns that migration is potentially destructive. Self-custody of rotation keys at signup is "technically possible but difficult and undocumented". A critique says PLC's exit story depends on third-party mirrors that may not be ready. Bluesky has announced moving did:plc governance and registry operation out of Bluesky PBC's sole control; completion is unconfirmed. — [bluesky-social/atproto discussion](https://github.com/bluesky-social/atproto/discussions/2151); [microcosm critique](https://updates.microcosm.blue/3lz7nwvh4zc2u); [IETF draft AT Architecture, Sept 2025](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-newbold-at-architecture-00.html)
- ActivityPub, Nostr, Matrix [background, not re-verified this session]:
  - **ActivityPub/Mastodon:** identity is bound to the server domain. If a server shuts down, accounts can't fully move: followers redirect, but posts don't move. Survival is by federation, not portability. — [W3C ActivityPub](https://www.w3.org/TR/activitypub/)
  - **Nostr:** identity is a keypair, and users publish signed events to many interchangeable relays. No server can kill an identity, but losing the key is unrecoverable and relays have no durability guarantee. — [NIP-01](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/01.md)
  - **Matrix:** rooms are replicated event DAGs across all participating homeservers, so a room survives any single server. Accounts are still homeserver-bound; MSCs on portable identity are long-pending. — [Matrix spec](https://spec.matrix.org/)

### Inferences (feasibility assessment)
- **Arithmetic.** 1,000 entities × 30 Hz is about 30K writes/s, and 60 Hz with 10K entities is about 600K/s. Even batching one tx per tick (30-60 tx/s of large state diffs) exceeds L1 and stresses any decentralized L2 on calldata and DA cost. Real-time chains that hit these numbers have one sequencer. The founder would be trading "a server we run" for "a sequencer someone runs".
- **What belongs on-chain (small, slow, high-value):**
  1. Account and identity anchors. Preferably DIDs (did:plc-like with user-held rotation keys, or did:ethr / smart-account addresses) and ERC-4337 or native account abstraction for social recovery and session keys, as Cartridge Controller does.
  2. Ownership of genuinely scarce items, if any, though Mojang's critique argues against scarcity.
  3. The world registry: canonical rule-set hash, client/server release hashes, list of federated shards.
  4. Periodic state commitments, such as a Merkle root of world state every N minutes.
  5. Dispute or upgrade logic.
- **What belongs in content-addressed storage:**
  - Full world snapshots, chunk data, assets and command-block or script programs, on IPFS, Filecoin or Arweave. CIDs are referenced from the on-chain commitment.
  - Arweave gives pay-once permanence. IPFS needs pinning by someone. Filecoin is a storage market, not permanent by default.
- **What stays off-chain:**
  - The 20-60 Hz authoritative simulation, physics, AI game-master (Cardinal) inference, chat and voice.
  - Make the simulation deterministic and open-source, so any party can replay inputs from the last snapshot and verify the committed root. This is the optimistic-rollup pattern applied to a game: fraud proofs or "anyone can re-execute and fork".
  - ZK-proving a real-time 3D simulation is impractical in 2026, except for narrow mechanics as Dark Forest showed.
  - LLM outputs are not deterministic or provable. Commit their outputs as inputs (a signed log), not their reasoning.
- **What makes it un-destroyable:**
  - An open license for code and content: code under (A)GPL or MIT, and content under CC-BY or a similar licence so anyone may re-host it.
  - Exportable state: public snapshots plus a deterministic replay log.
  - Portable identity, so users can follow a fork.
  - A federation of independent shard operators.
  - A minimal, rarely-changing on-chain core (credible neutrality).
  - No token voting over core rules. If governance is needed, use time-locks, non-transferable or reputation-based voting, and "rage-quit"/fork rights.
  - Redstone's death shows the anchor must be on a long-lived settlement layer, Ethereum L1 or a rollup posting data to L1, not a project-run appchain with alt-DA.
- **Federation lessons:**
  - Mastodon shows that server-bound identity makes users hostage to admins.
  - AT Protocol shows that a DID plus a signed data repo can be moved between hosts, with the caveat that the PLC directory is a soft central point.
  - Nostr shows that keys alone give censorship-resistance but poor recovery.
  - Matrix shows replicated shared state (rooms) surviving server loss, which is the closest analogue to a "world" that persists across operators.
  - The founder may get "no one can destroy it" largely without a blockchain: forkable OSS, a federated replicated world log, and portable DIDs. The chain adds a neutral timestamped registry and ownership ledger, which is useful but small.
- **Cost reality.** Anchoring one 32-byte root plus a CID every few minutes on an L2 is cheap, likely cents per day [estimate, not measured this session]. Writing every block change of a Minecraft-scale world on-chain is not.
